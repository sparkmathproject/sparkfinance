What is Valuation?

Valuation is what someone thinks a company is worth, like a price tag on the whole business.

The simplest way to calculate it: if someone asks for $50,000 for 20% of their company, they are saying the whole company is worth $250,000. That is $50,000 divided by 0.20.

But is it really worth that? You have to look at the numbers, revenue, growth, margins, to decide. Just because someone says their company is worth a million dollars does not mean it actually is.

Think About It: A company makes $10,000 a month and asks for $100,000 for 10%. That is a $1,000,000 valuation. With $120,000 annual revenue, would you pay $1,000,000 for it? That is over 8 times revenue, pretty expensive unless they are growing fast.

Try it yourself: An entrepreneur asks for $50,000 for 20% of their company. What is the implied valuation?

Equity, Royalties & Licensing

When you invest, you do not always take equity. There are three main deal types, and each one works differently.

Equity: Equity means you own a piece of the company. If it grows, your piece grows too. If it fails, you lose your money. Best for: High-growth companies you believe in.

Royalty Deal: A royalty deal means you get paid a small amount on every sale, like $1 per unit sold. Less risky because you get money back even if the company does not blow up. Best for: Steady businesses with lots of sales.

Licensing Deal: Licensing means the company pays you a fee to use something you provide, like your brand, your contacts, or your technology. Best for: When you are bringing something specific to the table.

Quick Cheat Sheet: Equity = you bet on the company's future. Big risk, big reward. Royalties = you get paid per sale. Safer, steadier returns. Licensing = they pay you for access to your stuff. Least risk.

Reading the Numbers

Three numbers tell you almost everything about a business:

Profit Margin: How much money the company keeps from each sale. A 50% margin means they keep 50 cents of every dollar. Under 20% is usually a red flag, they are working hard but barely keeping anything.

Growth Rate: How fast revenue is increasing month over month. 10 to 20% monthly growth is solid. Under 5% means the business might be stalling. Over 30% is exciting but ask yourself: can it last?

Customer Acquisition Cost: How much it costs to get one new customer. If it costs $50 to get a customer who only spends $30, that is a business that loses money the more it grows.

Red Flag Combos: High growth + low margins = burning cash. Flat growth + high ask = overvalued. Great margins + no growth = stuck.

The Art of the Deal

Rule 1: Never fall in love with a product. Fall in love with the numbers.

Rule 2: The entrepreneur's asking price is just a starting point. Most asks are 20 to 40% higher than fair value, they expect to negotiate.

Rule 3: Ask all your due diligence questions. The answers they dodge matter more than the answers they give.

Rule 4: Match your deal type to the business. High-volume, low-margin business? Royalties. High-growth tech? Equity. Niche product? Licensing.

Rule 5: Sometimes the best deal is walking away. Passing on a bad deal saves your fund for a great one.
